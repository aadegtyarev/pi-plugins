# Prose editing checklist

Use this during the editing pass. These are prompts to inspect, not automatic style rules; clarity and technical precision take priority.

## Common problems

1. **Trailing qualification.** A sentence finishes, then an em dash appends a second thought. Split the claims into sentences or state the condition first.
2. **Rationale in instructions.** Keep a how-to focused on action and result. Put the design reason in an explanation page and link to it.
3. **Bold labels on every bullet.** Emphasis is useful for scanning a reference, but noisy in every narrative list.
4. **Nested asides.** Replace stacked parentheses and asides with short sentences, a table, or a note.
5. **Padding.** Cut phrases such as “it is worth noting,” “in order to,” “seamlessly,” “robust,” and “simply” when they add no fact.
6. **Captions that repeat commands.** A caption should add context the command does not show, or be removed.
7. **Uniform rhythm.** Mix concise conclusions with denser explanations; do not repeat the same sentence pattern for every paragraph.
8. **Unstructured catalogues.** Put enumerable facts in tables or lists rather than burying them in prose.
9. **Marketing claims.** Replace “just works” or “powerful” with an observable behavior and its preconditions.
10. **Unsupported frequency or certainty.** State the conditions under which behavior occurs; do not imply measurements that are not available.

## English and Russian punctuation

In English, check whether an em dash is carrying a late hedge. A definition dash in a list is not the same thing.

In Russian, the copula dash is grammatically normal and is not a style defect:

- «Скилл — набор инструкций» — correct definition.
- «`alt`/`else` — ветвление по результату» — correct list separator.
- «Срабатывает при следующем опросе — не мгновенно, потому что…» — likely a trailing qualification; consider two sentences.

Do not use a raw dash count as a verdict. Read the sentence and preserve natural Russian grammar.

## Final pass

Read the page aloud. Check long sentences, repeated warnings, bold lead-ins, unnecessary parentheticals, and claims of completeness. Tools can point to suspicious patterns; a human must decide whether a rewrite preserves the technical meaning.
